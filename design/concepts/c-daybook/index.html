WorkScribe · interface concepts

Concept C · Day Book

WorkScribe as a ship's log of your working day. There is one page per day, and everything lands on it in time order: calls, your jots, the notes they turn into, actions, files you opened, web clips, questions you asked and the answers you got. You always type on the now line, whether you're in a call or not, and bullet-journal signifiers (• ! ? →) decide what a line becomes. The assistant never gets a panel of its own. It writes in the margin, beside the line it's about: the excerpts a note came from, a live answer, “you promised Priya this at 10:29”, the evening digest. Ask is just a “?” jot answered in the margin. Search puts moments back on the days they happened. Pages are Markdown in your Obsidian vault.

Start
1 · MorningThe page ahead: carried-over items, a clip, a “?” jot answered, and calls pencilled in with margin prep. 2 · Live callThe call is a section of the day. Jots enhance in place; evidence, a live answer and a missed topic sit in the margin. 3 · EveningThe day folded to one line per thing, a four-line digest, promises found in your own voice, and the action ledger. 4 · MomentsSearch results as moments placed back on their days, with a month ribbon and Ask in the margin.

Best at

  • One gesture for everything. Jotting in a call, asking a question and noting a thought at 8am are the same act on the same line, so the jotting habit spreads to the whole day.
  • Context by adjacency. The 10:16 call sits under the 08:44 clip and the 08:47 answer; the margin can connect them because they're neighbours in time.
  • Recall by “when”. People remember “the morning of the site visit” better than file names. Results grouped by day read as a story.
  • Promises and the daily ritual. The digest, found promises and carry-to-tomorrow give a reason to open the app every evening.
  • Plays well with Obsidian. A day page is a daily note; calls and clips are linked notes.

Worst at

  • Project-shaped work. Everything about Halvorsen is scattered across many days. You need search or a “thread” view to see a topic whole, and that's a second organising idea fighting the first.
  • Long calls crowd the day. A 90-minute call with 30 jots makes the page mostly one meeting; collapsing helps but the day stops reading as a day.
  • The margin is narrow. Long answers and evidence for many notes compete for one column; on small windows the margin folds under its line and the page gets long.
  • “Ahead” needs a calendar. Morning prep depends on opt-in EventKit, which WorkScribe doesn't have today.
