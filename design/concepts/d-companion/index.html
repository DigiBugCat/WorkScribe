WorkScribe · interface concepts

Concept D · Companion

The whole app is one ongoing conversation.

OverviewMorningLive callAfter the callAsk · moments

WorkScribe is a colleague you talk to in one long thread. It sat in every meeting and read every file you pointed it at, so the thread holds your day: a brief in the morning, a nudge before a call, the call itself as a live block you jot into, the synthesis after, and any question you ask, answered with moments you can open beside the conversation. It isn’t a generic chatbot because almost nothing it says is a paragraph. Replies are typed blocks (brief, promises, decisions, action items, moments) that you pin, open in a side sheet, or reply to in order to change. Threads are just filters of the same thread (a customer, a project, a person). Your notes live as Markdown in your Obsidian vault, and web clips arrive there too.

Best at

  • Continuity. Yesterday’s promise, this morning’s clip and the 14:00 call sit in one scroll, so the nudge “here’s what you promised” makes sense without explanation.
  • Proactive help that doesn’t need a dashboard. Timed messages replace a home screen.
  • Follow-ups. Every result is something you can reply to (“only what Ingrid said”, “draft it for Ingrid”), so search, Ask and editing are the same gesture.
  • Jotting stays terse. Jot mode files lines into the live block with no replies, and the enhancement lands on the same row.

Worst at

  • Finding things by browsing. A chronological stream buries last month’s meeting; you depend on asking, pins and threads instead of a list you can scan.
  • Mode confusion during calls. One composer with Ask and Jot modes can send a question as a note or the reverse; the mode has to be very loud.
  • Noise. If proactive messages aren’t strictly tied to a clock or a due item, the conversation becomes a feed you skim past.
  • Long documents. Notes and transcripts are cramped as blocks; they only really work in the sheet or in Obsidian.
