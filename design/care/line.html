The AI line

Your words are ink. WorkScribe’s are pencil: the same typeface, a little smaller, a little lighter, sitting directly under the line they belong to. Pencil can be rubbed out and rewritten. At Finalize it is inked.

Anatomy

What it is
A CodeMirror block decoration after your line. It is not document text, so undo, search and sync only ever see what you typed.
Indent
Aligned to your text, not to the bullet. It reads as the second half of your thought.
Type
The theme’s --font-text at 0.9375em (15 px under 16 px), line height 1.5. Your note names the topic; the pencil line adds only the detail.
Colour
--text-muted. No WorkScribe colour, font or icon enters your editor; community themes just work.
Space
1 px above, 7 px to your next line, so each pair reads as one unit.
Mark
None when settled. A 4 px nib in the bullet column only while something is happening: hollow while listening, breathing while drafting, a dash while paused.
Hover
Why? in the right margin opens the transcript at the moments it came from, until the transcript fades.
Edit
Click into it and type. It becomes yours on the first keystroke. No accept, no dismiss.

Seven states

Each one is a decision about how much to say. The default is to say less.

A rewrite, slowed down

The rewrite is the moment the product is judged on. It should feel like someone quietly correcting a pencil note, not like a page reloading.

With Reduce Motion on, nothing fades or slides: the words change in one step and the new ones keep their darker colour for 2.4 s, so the change is still visible.

When a line may rewrite

  1. Only when the facts change. The model gets its previous line and must return it unchanged unless new evidence adds or contradicts something. A rewrite that only rephrases is dropped before it reaches the screen.
  2. Never under the line you’re typing. A new line waits for Return or a 4 s pause before anything appears under it.
  3. Not near your hands. Within three lines of the cursor, rewrites wait until you’ve stopped typing for 2 s.
  4. Not often. A 4 s debounce on new transcript, and at most one pass every 15 s per meeting.
  5. Your spelling wins. Numbers, names and acronyms you typed survive exactly as you typed them, unless the transcript plainly corrects them.
  6. No flapping. A line can’t lose more than half its words without new evidence, and can’t return to a wording it had before.
  7. Animate only what you can see. Lines scrolled out of view update silently. When an AI line above the cursor grows, the editor scrolls by the same amount, so your line doesn’t move.
  8. If you edit your own line, its AI line dims and rereads (“Rereading…”) rather than disappearing, then rewrites once against your new words.

Its final form in the note

At Finalize the pencil becomes Markdown. Three candidates, shown as source and as Obsidian renders them.

Callout No

- ship date nov 4 not oct 28
  > [!workscribe]
  > v2.4 moves from Oct 28 to Nov 4
  > so QA gets a full week on the
  > SQLite migration.
- android?
ship date nov 4 not oct 28
Workscribe
v2.4 moves from Oct 28 to Nov 4 so QA gets a full week on the SQLite migration.
android?

For: unmistakable; can be collapsed; styled by a CSS snippet.

Against: seven lines become seven boxes. It needs a custom callout type that means nothing outside Obsidian, and the plain text is noisy with > [!…].

Blockquote No

- ship date nov 4 not oct 28
  > v2.4 moves from Oct 28 to Nov 4
  > so QA gets a full week on the
  > SQLite migration.
- android?
ship date nov 4 not oct 28
v2.4 moves from Oct 28 to Nov 4 so QA gets a full week on the SQLite migration.
android?

For: portable and familiar.

Against: a quote mark means “someone said this”, and nobody did. In a note full of real quotes from Priya and Marcus, it would be read as one.

Indented italic Pick

- ship date nov 4 not oct 28
  *v2.4 moves from Oct 28 to Nov 4 so
  QA gets a full week on the SQLite
  migration. Priya tells Halvorsen on
  Friday.*
- android?
ship date nov 4 not oct 28
v2.4 moves from Oct 28 to Nov 4 so QA gets a full week on the SQLite migration. Priya tells Halvorsen on Friday.
android?

For: the lightest mark that survives every renderer: Obsidian, GitHub, iA Writer, a plain-text email. Italic has long meant another voice: stage directions, marginalia, a translator’s note. It stays inside your bullet, so outlines fold correctly.

Caveat: strict CommonMark joins the two lines with a space. Obsidian’s default (strict line breaks off) shows the break.

What gets written, exactly.

  • The topic lead is dropped. Your line above already names the topic (“ship date nov 4 not oct 28” doesn’t need “Ship date.” under it).
  • Lines you took over are written without italics. A week later you can still tell your words from WorkScribe’s, with no metadata and no plugin.
  • A line that was never discussed gets nothing written under it. Your “android?” stays a question.
  • Asterisks and underscores inside the AI text are escaped, so a line about *.heic files can’t break the italics.
  • No block IDs, transcript links or evidence anchors survive Finalize. The note has to stand on its own after the transcript fades.
