Ink and pencil

Only the note survives a WorkScribe meeting. So the note is the product, and everything else should be as quiet as a pencil line under your own words.

Your words are ink. WorkScribe writes in pencil underneath, and keeps correcting itself while the conversation runs. At the end you ink it, and the tape is thrown away.

Three shapes

The same capabilities can be arranged in very different ways. These are the three most different arrangements I could make honest, each with its menu bar, its Obsidian and its Ask.

AChosen

Pencil in the note

WorkScribe lives inside the note you’re already writing. Under each of your lines, a pencil line rewrites itself as the meeting goes. The app itself is a tally light.

Menu bar
A tally: status, Stop, Quick ask, what’s fading.
Obsidian
Everything you read and write, live and after.
Ask
The right sidebar, scoped to the open note; Quick ask is the same chat, anywhere.

Best at: trust in the moment; the note you keep.

Worst at: people who don’t type in meetings, and anything that isn’t a meeting.

B

The desk

A place where the day collects: meetings, clips, questions heard, answers given, ambient captures. You clear it by filing things into notes.

Menu bar
An inbox count: what landed on the desk.
Obsidian
Where filed things end up; a second room.
Ask
At the desk, over the day’s pile.

Best at: loose things with no note to live in; ambient capture.

Worst at: it fights the fade. A desk of things that delete themselves in 24 hours is a desk of countdowns, and an inbox that needs emptying is a chore.

C

The familiar

Mostly invisible. It listens, answers when asked, nudges at the right moment, and leaves a finished note behind. You rarely watch it work.

Menu bar
Everything: status, nudges, a small feed.
Obsidian
The output folder.
Ask
Quick ask only.

Best at: zero attention; people who just want the minutes.

Worst at: it hides the live line, which is what earns trust. You’d judge it only by its output, at the end, when it’s too late to steer.

Why the note

Care belongs where things last. Transcript, audio, screen and evidence are gone in a day. The note is the only thing you’ll ever see again, so it gets the craft, and every other surface gets as little as it can bear.

The live line is the idea. Reading across two columns takes extra effort. A pencil line directly under your own words turns the meeting into a quiet conversation with your notes, and lets you steer while it’s still happening.

Obsidian is already open. A second window would compete for the same screen next to Zoom. Inside Obsidian, WorkScribe wears Obsidian’s clothes: its font, its colours, its sidebar.

What it borrows. From the familiar: the tally, so you know it’s listening without looking. From the desk: nothing structural. Loose things (a clip outside a meeting, an answer you keep) go to today’s daily note, which is already your desk.

People who don’t type still get something: a meeting with an empty note ends with a brief, “Also discussed” and action items. That is the familiar’s whole output, for free.

Ambient capture (screenpipe-style, gated by a decision model) gets no surface at all in this shape. It is a source Ask can cite, under the same 24-hour fade. No feed, no timeline. If it ever needs a surface, it’s a line in the daily note, written only when you ask.

The risk is mostly engineering: CodeMirror decorations that rewrite live must handle IME, scroll anchoring and Obsidian Sync without a flicker. The mockups assume that bar is met.

Decisions for you

  1. Finalize: a press, or automatic? I chose an explicit Finalize with a safety net (it happens on its own an hour before the transcript fades). The alternative writes into the note as soon as the final pass ends, which is simpler but takes away the moment where you remove what you don’t want.Recommendation: explicit, with the safety net.
  2. Indented italic as the final form, with edited lines left roman and topic labels dropped. It reads beautifully in Obsidian; strict CommonMark renderers join the two lines with a space.Recommendation: accept the caveat.
  3. Where Indigo Wash still lives. Inside Obsidian, WorkScribe uses the theme’s own tokens. I also made the menu, notifications and Quick ask fully native. That leaves Indigo Wash for the app icon, onboarding and the website.Recommendation: yes, retire it from in-product surfaces.
  4. Strip section markers at the fade. The vault writer’s <!-- workscribe:begin --> markers protect your edits while WorkScribe might still write. After the fade it never will, so they can go, leaving pure Markdown. The cost: no later regeneration of that note, ever.Recommendation: strip them.
  5. Notes before the call. Creating the note four minutes ahead (so prep lines get answered) needs calendar access through EventKit.Recommendation: ask for it at the first meeting, not during onboarding.
  6. The start tick. One sound, only when recording starts by itself. On by default?Recommendation: on; it’s the only way to know auto-start heard the call.
