The menu bar and the edges

Outside Obsidian, WorkScribe is a tally light: you should be able to tell what it’s doing from the corner of your eye, without opening anything. Everything here is native: a real NSMenu, real notifications, and a Quick ask panel that behaves like Spotlight.

The tally

Five states. Each has a different shape, not just a different colour, so it reads at a glance and in grayscale.

The menu

A plain menu, not a popover. It answers three questions: is it recording, what can I do right now, and what’s about to fade.

Between meetings

The header is the next meeting and how far away it is. “Open Meeting Note” opens the note before the call, so you can write prep lines; the meeting will answer them.

Stop and save “Offline sync — v2.4 scope”, then quit?Recording stops and the final pass finishes before WorkScribe quits. Your note is safe either way.
CancelStop and Quit

Quit while recording

Quitting never silently ends a recording. The default button says exactly what will happen. Updates wait until no meeting is recording.

What the menu bar is for. Status, Stop, and the things you need when Obsidian isn’t in front: Quick ask, open the note, see what’s fading. It is not a second home for the product, so it has no transcript, no answers and no settings panes of its own.

What it never does. It never animates while idle, never shows a count badge, and never changes width while recording. The timer slot is sized for 00:00 from the start and widens once, at the hour.

Sound. One soft tick when recording starts by itself (auto-start on a detected call), because that is the only time you didn’t press anything. Manual starts and stops are silent. There is no haptic: a Mac trackpad you aren’t touching can’t tell you anything.

Notifications

Three kinds, each with one action. Everything else waits for you to look.

Quick ask

⌥⌘K from anywhere, including over the call. It opens already scoped to the meeting in progress, answers in a few lines with sources, and gets out of the way. It is the same conversation as Ask in Obsidian’s side panel.

Scoped before you ask

The chip says what it will search: the live meeting first, then your vault. One click on ✕ widens it to everything.

Where it was answered

Every answer says which on-device model made it. Nothing is sent off this Mac.

Nothing on the shared screen

Quick ask opens only when you call it. It never appears by itself, so it can’t surprise you during a screen share.

Esc forgets nothing

Closing keeps a half-typed question for ten minutes. ⌘↩ inserts the answer under your cursor in the note, as your line, with its source.

The clipper

The Chrome extension clips into the meeting in progress. It says which meeting before you press anything.
